Recitation is an optional extension, independent of the commentary package. Preferences lets readers choose all books or individual books, download them for offline playback, cancel and resume downloads, and separately enable recitation in the reader.

The extension catalog comes from the website's `/api/recitation` route, generated from the canonical perakim database. The app writes a complete new SQLite database and atomically replaces `extensions/recitation/recitation.sqlite`, allowing reinstallation to repair a damaged catalog. Original MP3s live beside it under their SHA-256 identities. Successful downloads are retained for retries and metadata updates; files with incorrect hashes are never installed. Installed catalogs refresh at most daily when the reader opens, retaining the previous catalog when offline.

With recitation enabled, holding the chapter header opens a toolbar with only Back and Recitation. Holding verses retains the selection counter, Copy and Share, and adds Recitation to the right of Share. A rapid double tap opens a focused verse with its available commentary, independent of whether recitation is enabled. Approved words become tappable when the recording is installed. The playback button pauses and resumes, and navigation, disabling the extension, or backgrounding the app stops playback.

Chapter playback uses the original local MP3. Words and verses use complete-track MPEG decoding, then exact approved PCM sample ranges in a temporary WAV for native playback. The pinned mpg123 decoder is identical to the website's decoder and runs locally in the WebView without remote content or browser audio output. Both directions of its bridge are chunked to avoid mobile message limits. It caches only the current decoded chapter and releases that audio on navigation. No start padding or approximate MP3 seeks are used. Pending chapters support their full recording but expose no word intervals. Canonical identities, text hashes, source audio hashes, and approved interval boundaries are checked before segment playback.

Website CD verifies the complete app extension against the canonical DB as well as checking approved chapter APIs and audio. Decoder changes trigger website CI's independent FFmpeg-reference browser regression. The recitation service tests cover partial installation, offline use, resume, integrity failures, rejected updates, canonical mismatches, and pending chapter restrictions.

App CI compares all 929 native chapters' spoken word identities with the alignment database, including segment positions across null-valued nonspoken markers. The bundled native DB includes the same repaired Eikhah 5:22 and Kohelet 12:14 words as the website. The existing packaged-database refresh installs corrected text for users upgrading from older app versions.

The macOS iOS CI runner also verifies the decoder against independent FFmpeg PCM on Chromium and Apple WebKit, including the unchanged original Esther 10 recording. The portable decoder preserves the FFmpeg time origin on every platform; platform MP3 decoding is not used for segmentation. The browser regression also checks that the native asset is reproducible from the exact website dependency lock. To regenerate it, run `node scripts/build-native-recitation-decoder.mjs --write` in `web/bible-on-site`. The browser check requires no server or database. Decoder dependency changes trigger both app and website CI. Third-party notices, licenses, and pinned upstream source links are bundled beside the decoder.
